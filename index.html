Amelie Tolvin

PhD Candidate, Political Science

I am a PhD Candidate in Political Science at the University of Toronto and a researcher in the Mannerheim Professorship of Russian Security Studies Working Group at the University of Helsinki.

My doctoral dissertation examines variation in violence against civilians during Russia's invasion of Ukraine, asking why some hromadas under Russian occupation experienced more frequent violence than others. I bridge strategic and operational explanations of this violence, arguing that military unit-level dynamics are the primary driver of variation. This research takes a mixed-methods approach: a quantitative analysis based on an original dataset of violent events against civilians, and a qualitative process-tracing analysis drawing on existing, published testimonies from Ukrainian civilians.

More broadly, my research interests include authoritarian regimes, coercive institutions, and repression, with a particular focus on Russia and Belarus. I have been published in the Journal of Slavic Military Studies, Nationalities Papers, Foreign Policy, and the Moscow Times. I have also contributed a chapter to the Oxford Handbook of Authoritarian Politics (2024, Oxford University Press).

I am fortunate to have my research funded by the Social Science & Humanities Research Council of Canada, as well as the Dr Roman Turko and Yaroslawa Turko and Dmytro and Natalia Haluszka Family Scholarships in Ukrainian Studies.
